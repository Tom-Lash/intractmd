The Proactive Risk Score (0–100) measures how many clinically significant drug-drug interactions, supplement risks, and food risks exist for your specific medication regimen. A higher score means more interactions requiring attention.

0–20 Minimal — Few or no significant interactions identified. Standard precautions apply.
21–40 Low — Minor interactions present. Review warnings and discuss with your pharmacist.
41–60 Moderate — Several interactions identified. Some supplements or foods should be avoided. Consult your doctor.
61–80 High — Multiple significant interactions. Medication review recommended. Avoid flagged supplements and foods.
81–100 Critical — Serious interactions present. Contact your doctor or pharmacist immediately before taking any supplements.

This tool provides educational information only and is not a substitute for professional medical advice. Always consult your healthcare provider before making changes to your medications or supplements.

Risk by dimension
Bleeding Risk - Anticoagulant and antiplatelet burden from warfarin, aspirin, NSAIDs, SSRIs, fish oil, vitamin E.
Cardiac Risk - QT prolongation risk from antiarrhythmics, antidepressants, antibiotics, bitter orange.
Serotonin Risk - Combined serotonergic burden from SSRIs, MAOIs, tramadol, St. Johns Wort, 5-HTP.
NTI Conflict - Interactions involving narrow therapeutic index drugs: warfarin, digoxin, lithium, levothyroxine.
CNS Risk - Additive sedation from opioids, benzodiazepines, antihistamines, alcohol, valerian, kava.
CYP450 Risk - Enzyme-mediated metabolism interactions. Grapefruit juice and St. Johns Wort are common triggers.
Renal/Hepatic - Cumulative kidney and liver stress from NSAIDs, kava, comfrey, high-dose acetaminophen.
Pharmacodynamic - Additive blood pressure, blood sugar, and absorption effects not covered above.
